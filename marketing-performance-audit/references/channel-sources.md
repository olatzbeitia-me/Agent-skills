# Channel sources: where to look and what each source can and cannot tell you

Checked November 2026. Transparency tools change often, and most of their detail is driven by EU law (the Digital Services Act), so coverage differs sharply between EU and non-EU markets. If a tool behaves differently from what is described here, trust what you see and note the difference in the briefing.

## 1. Access: how to actually read these tools

Most ad libraries are script-heavy search apps. A plain page fetch often returns an empty shell.

1. Try the page-fetch tool on a direct search URL first.
2. If it returns nothing useful and the session has a browser tool, use the browser to run the search and read the results.
3. If neither works, do not guess. Record the channel as "not checked: tool not reachable from this session", and give the user the exact library and the search to run (advertiser name, country filter, date range) so they can check it in two minutes.

Say which channels you could and could not reach near the top of the briefing, not buried at the end.

## 2. Paid media: public ad libraries

### LinkedIn Ad Library — linkedin.com/ad-library
- **Search by:** company or advertiser, payer, keyword, country, date.
- **Shows for all ads:** creative, copy, format, advertiser and payer.
- **Shows only for ads whose targeting includes the EU:** run dates, an impressions band, the split of impressions by country, and the targeting the advertiser chose.
- **Practical tip:** set the country filter to an EU market (Germany, Ireland, Netherlands) to surface dates and impressions for global campaigns. A UK-only or US-only search shows creative with no dates, so every ad looks the same age.
- **Watch for:** loose advertiser matching (partners and resellers with similar names appear). Confirm the advertiser page. The payer field can reveal the agency running the account.
- **Most useful for:** business-to-business offers, audience targeting by job function or seniority (EU ads), and how long a creative has survived.

### Google Ads Transparency Center — adstransparency.google.com
- **Search by:** advertiser name or website only. There is no keyword search.
- **Covers:** ads served on Google platforms; Google's help names Search, Display, Gmail and YouTube as examples. Google does not publish a fixed look-back period (its help says only "a certain time period"), so record the earliest date the tool shows rather than assuming a window.
- **Shows:** creative and its variations, format, the date an ad was last shown, regions shown, and the advertiser's name, location and payer, with a verified or unverified badge.
- **Shows only for ads in certain countries, including European Union member states:** targeting information, the total number of recipients of each ad, and a subject label that Google sometimes generates. Select one of these countries to see it.
- **Does not show:** keywords, bids, budgets or the search terms that triggered an ad. Never claim to know a company's keywords from this tool; ad copy only hints at the topic.
- **Watch for:** ads listed under a legal entity or agency payer name. If the brand name returns nothing, try the domain and the registered company name before concluding there are no ads.

### Meta Ad Library — facebook.com/ads/library
- **Covers:** Facebook, Instagram, Messenger and Audience Network.
- **Shows:** every currently active ad, worldwide, with creative, copy, platforms and start date.
- **Inactive commercial ads:** only visible if they were delivered in the EU, kept for a year after their last impression, with reach and targeting data.
- **Political and social-issue ads:** kept for seven years, but Meta stopped accepting these in the EU from October 2025.
- **Implication:** for a UK-only or US-only advertiser you see what is running today and nothing about what ran before. A thin result can mean "nothing running this week", not "never advertises here".

### Microsoft Ad Library — adlibrary.ads.microsoft.com
- **Covers:** only ads served on Bing that received impressions in the EU or European Economic Area.
- **Use when:** the company sells into Europe. Absence here says nothing about UK or US Bing activity.

### TikTok Commercial Content Library — library.tiktok.com
- **No account needed.** Two parts: the Ad Library, and "Other commercial content" (creator posts labelled as paid partnerships).
- **Data covers Europe only.** Shows first shown, last shown and unique users reached.
- **Separate tool:** TikTok Creative Center shows trending top-performing ads across advertisers. It is a category benchmark, not a company lookup.

### X ads repository
- EU only and known to be incomplete (the European Commission fined X in December 2025, partly over gaps in its repository). Low value; mention only if X looks like a real channel for the company.

### Signals of paid activity outside the libraries
- Landing pages with tracking parameters in the URL (campaign names often reveal audience, offer and channel).
- Dedicated "lp", "go" or "get" subdomains, or no-index landing pages linked from ads.
- Tracking tags on the website (LinkedIn Insight Tag, Meta Pixel, Google tag) show which platforms the company is set up to advertise on, even when no ads are visible. Readable only if you can view page source, usually through a browser tool.

## 3. Organic social

For each platform the company is active on, capture: follower count, posting frequency over the last 30 to 90 days, content themes, formats (video, carousel, document, text), and engagement relative to audience size.

- **LinkedIn company page:** often behind a sign-in wall for fetch tools. Search results and cached snippets can still show follower counts and recent posts.
- **Executive and employee voices:** in business-to-business markets, founders' and senior leaders' personal posts frequently outperform the company page. Check the chief executive and the marketing leader.
- **YouTube:** subscriber count, upload cadence, view counts relative to subscribers, whether videos are product demos, webinars or thought leadership.
- **Instagram, TikTok, X, Facebook:** check only where the audience plausibly is; absence is a finding only if competitors in the same industry are active there.

Engagement read: a high follower count with single-digit reactions per post usually means a bought or legacy audience, or content the audience ignores. Say which.

## 4. Website, landing pages and search

- **Homepage and positioning:** headline promise, who it names as the customer, proof (logos, numbers, awards), primary call to action (demo, free trial, contact sales, pricing).
- **Conversion path:** how many steps from homepage to a sales conversation or sign-up; form length; whether pricing is public. This tells you whether the motion is product-led, sales-led or hybrid.
- **Content engine:** blog or resource hub publishing cadence (read dates on the index page), gated content (reports, guides), webinars, newsletters.
- **Search presence without paid tools:** run searches for the company's core category terms and note whether the company appears on page one; run "site:" searches to gauge the size of the content library; check whether competitors appear for the company's own brand name.
- **Share of search:** a Google Trends comparison of the company against its competitor set. Follow the method and limits in `demand-proxies.md`; low-volume brands show as 0 and are recorded as not measurable.
- **Messaging history:** the Wayback Machine (web.archive.org) shows how the homepage headline and positioning have changed. A recent repositioning is a strong strategic signal.

## 5. Third-party coverage

- **Press and trade media:** the company newsroom, then news searches and the trade titles for the industry given. Separate earned coverage from paid placements and press-release syndication.
- **Funding and corporate news:** funding rounds, acquisitions, leadership hires (a new marketing leader often precedes a change in strategy). For UK companies, Companies House filings give registered names, officers and, for larger firms, revenue and headcount.
- **Reviews:** G2, Capterra, Trustpilot, Gartner Peer Insights, depending on industry. Look at volume, recency and rating trend. A sudden burst of reviews in one period usually means a review-collection campaign.
- **Podcasts:** guest appearances by leaders (search the leader's name plus "podcast"), and any company-owned show.
- **Forums and communities:** Reddit, industry Slack or Discord groups, specialist forums. Look for unprompted mentions, complaints and comparisons with competitors.
- **Analyst coverage:** mentions in analyst reports or market maps signal category credibility, even when the report itself is paywalled.

## 6. Other demand signals

- **Job postings:** one of the most revealing public sources. Marketing roles show the tool stack, the channels being invested in, team structure and priorities ("Paid Social Manager, first hire" says more than any ad library). Check the careers page and job boards; use a jobs connector if the session has one.
- **Technology stack:** marketing automation, chat, intent data and account-based tools visible on the site or in job ads.
- **Events:** own events, webinars, conference sponsorships and speaking slots (check sponsor lists of the main industry conferences).
- **Partnerships:** integration marketplaces, co-marketing, referral or affiliate programmes, reseller networks.

## 7. Paywalled tools: what to say when you cannot reach them

Similarweb (traffic and channel mix estimates), Semrush and Ahrefs (organic and paid search estimates) and paid ad-intelligence tools are mostly behind paywalls. Do not cite figures you have not seen. Instead, name the tool and the specific report that would close the gap, for example "Semrush organic keywords report for the domain would confirm whether category rankings are growing".
