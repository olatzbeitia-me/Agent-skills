## What changed

1. **Company and deal sync brought up to date (outdated platform facts, new guardrail).** HubSpot now runs an updated company sync and an updated deal sync next to the standard ones. The skill said companies match only through associated contacts, that deals are never de-duplicated, and that company and deal mappings have no "Always use HubSpot" rule; all 3 are now true only of the standard syncs. `references/integration-sync.md` now describes both versions: what the updated syncs add (matching on identical mapped fields, object-level create, update and delete rules, a first-match conflict rule, association sync, HubSpot-favouring field rules that work only with a two-way object rule, selective sync filters, record-type pipeline eligibility for deals), that merges in HubSpot never merge Salesforce records, and that neither update can be switched back. Switching one on is now listed as a large-blast-radius change with a pre-flight (sandbox test, clean the matching field, check API headroom). The sync rules section now says which rules exist per object, the duplicates section is corrected, and 2 mapping gotchas are added (no duplicate or overlapping mappings; bulk company resyncs cap at 200,000). The IP restriction advice now names the app's Relax IP Restrictions setting. `SKILL.md` asks whether the updated syncs are on when it matters, adds the switch to its blast-radius list, and adds a dated fact.
2. **Limiting what syncs from Salesforce, and the consent gap it creates (new method step and guardrail).** The skill covered the inclusion segment (HubSpot to Salesforce) but not the other direction. A new subsection explains selective sync: field filters for accounts and opportunities on the updated syncs, and, for leads and contacts, HubSpot's sharing-rule method with its limits (Salesforce Enterprise, unsupported by HubSpot Support, integration-user-owned records always sync). It recommends filtering only on a clear rule and warns that a record hidden from HubSpot stops receiving HubSpot opt-outs. `references/hubspot-inbound.md` gains the documented limits of opt-out sync: it applies only to syncing contacts, clearing Email Opt Out in Salesforce never resubscribes, and HubSpot sends only "opted out" or blank. Migration test script 5 now checks the no-resubscribe behaviour.
3. **One-time transfer versus ongoing sync (new guardrail in the migration playbook).** HubSpot's Smart Transfer is its tool for a one-time move off Salesforce; the connector is for an ongoing sync, and the two identify records differently, so using both on the same data duplicates records in both systems. The playbook gains a short section on choosing the route, with Smart Transfer's practical limits (Super Admins only, Data Hub for custom field mappings, auto-enabled bridging workflows, a background sync that keeps running, a revert option for the rollback plan). Scenario C's risk, the build phase and the connector prerequisites reflect it, and the playbook flags that HubSpot does not say whether the duplicate warning applies to Smart Transfer from Account Engagement.

`SKILL.md` grows from 90 to 92 lines; the detail sits in `references/`.

## Why

- HubSpot's company sync article (updated 17 September 2026) and deal sync article (updated 6 October 2026) describe the updated syncs, their extra rules and filters, and say neither can be reverted. An architect who advises on de-duplication or a HubSpot-owned company field without asking which version is in use will give a wrong configuration, and switching on either version blind is an irreversible change to how records match.
- HubSpot's planning guide (updated 16 October 2026) names selective sync as the way to limit what comes from Salesforce. Its leads and contacts article (updated 31 August 2026) says opt-out status does not sync for a contact that is not syncing, and that clearing the Salesforce opt-out does not resubscribe. Together these mean any filter on the Salesforce side is also a consent decision.
- HubSpot's connection article (updated 5 November 2026) and Smart Transfer article (updated 10 November 2026) both warn that Smart Transfer and the connector must not run together on Salesforce data. The migration playbook covered consolidation from Salesforce to HubSpot without mentioning the tool HubSpot built for it.
- Anthropic's skill authoring guidance keeps `SKILL.md` as a short overview with detail one level down in reference files, which is why the new material sits in `references/` and `SKILL.md` gains only routing, questions and dated facts.

## Considered and rejected

- **Recommending a default "switch on the updated syncs".** The articles describe features, not who should use them; whether the gains outweigh an irreversible change depends on the client's data. The skill recommends testing and a recorded decision instead.
- **Adding HubSpot's 2 activity sync modes (one-way timeline sync and two-way activity sync).** The planning guide mentions them, but this run did not read the activity sync article itself; left for a later review rather than described from a summary.
- **Recording that HubSpot-Salesforce campaign sync has launched.** No HubSpot documentation found says so; the October 2026 planning guide still says Salesforce campaigns have no direct HubSpot equivalent. The `SKILL.md` fact now cites that and keeps the instruction to check status.
- **Settling whether a cheaper Salesforce integration licence works for the connected user.** HubSpot's November 2026 requirements still ask for a system administrator; no source found addresses the integration licence, so the existing "check before budgeting" line stays.
- **Salesforce's 2025 restrictions on uninstalled connected apps.** Relevant to connector security, but no HubSpot or Salesforce page found ties them to the HubSpot connector; not added without a source.
- **Third-party implementation guides found in search.** Partner and agency blogs were skipped in favour of HubSpot's own documentation.
