# 2026-11-18: field-marketing-event-strategy

**Date:** 18 November 2026
**Agent:** [field-marketing-event-strategy](../field-marketing-event-strategy/)
**Why this agent:** 10 agents had never been reviewed; broken alphabetically, this one comes first. No `improve/` pull requests were open.

## What changed

1. **An in-market check in the event economics.** Step 1 now asks for the purchase interval (how often a typical customer buys, or reviews its supplier for, this kind of product), and `references/research-playbook.md` says where to find it. Step 2 then tests the qualified opportunities an event needs against how many new target accounts can be in the market during the reporting window. The new reference works this through on the skill's existing £60,000 trade show illustration: 40 new target accounts and a 4-year purchase interval give about 2.5 accounts in the market within 3 months and 10 within 12 months, against 6 to 10 opportunities required. An event that cannot clear that bar is downgraded or given a stated longer-term job.
2. **Buying groups as the unit of planning and measurement.** The event thesis names the buying-group roles the event must reach (Forrester's 5: champion, influencer, decision-maker, user and ratifier). "Buyer match" in the scoring table now considers how many roles per account an event can reach. The lead capture form records buying role alongside persona; a new "deduplicate people, not accounts" rule stops later contacts from the same account being discarded; buying-group coverage becomes a leading indicator and part of the first-week report; and the agreement with sales includes the buying-group profile.
3. **Reporting windows set by the event's job, and attribution settings that can see them.** Step 8 sets the window by job (within a quarter for accelerating open deals, 6 to 12 months or longer for new accounts, renewal dates for expansion) and compares against comparable accounts the team did not meet. `references/sales-plays.md` adds a 12-month review for new-account events, adding event contacts to opportunities with their buying role, and checking the customer relationship management system's influence window. The back-scheduled timeline in `references/contracts-and-vendors.md` gains 6- and 12-month reviews.
4. **New reference file.** `references/measurement.md` holds the method, the worked illustration, the cautions, the limits of the evidence and the sources. `SKILL.md` points to it and grows from 170 to 177 lines.

## Why

- Step 2 worked out how many qualified opportunities an event must create but never asked whether that many accounts could be buying. Dawes (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute) shows that where companies replace a supplier about every 5 years, only about 20% are in the market in a year and about 5% in a quarter, and gives a simple way to calculate the share for any purchase interval. Judged at 90 days, a new-account event is mostly judged on accounts that could not buy yet.
- The skill mentioned the buying group in its research step but then planned, captured and reported by person. Forrester's 2026 buyer research puts the average purchase at 13 internal stakeholders and 9 external participants, with procurement a decision-maker in 53% of buying cycles. Forrester's 2023 series on moving away from marketing qualified leads separates persona from buying role, names the 5 roles, and warns that later contacts from the same account are often discarded as duplicates.
- Forrester's event research points the same way. Mills (2024) warns against treating one attendee as the single source of an opportunity and notes that enterprise opportunities can take years, so in-year return calculations understate events. Mills (2026) found about half of event leaders satisfied with performance against about a third of chief marketing officers, with leading programmes reporting contribution to revenue, customer growth and buying-group engagement. Forrester's earlier SiriusDecisions guidance (2017) gives the windows: a quarter for accelerating late-stage deals, 6 to 12 months for early-stage demand creation.
- The skill told users to set up the event campaign before outreach, which in Salesforce starts the influence clock at invitation. Salesforce's documentation confirms that automatic influence works through campaign members who also hold a contact role on an open opportunity, that the time frame runs from campaign membership to opportunity creation, and that every member counts whatever their status unless the rules filter it. A team following the skill with a short time frame, or without contact roles, would undercount the event.

## Considered and rejected

- **Sharper UK rules for business email follow-up.** The Information Commissioner's Office confirms that the electronic mail rule in the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations does not apply to corporate subscribers, while the UK General Data Protection Regulation still does. The skill's existing wording ("consent or another lawful basis") is correct at its level of detail, and the regulator says its business-to-business guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. Adding detail now risks going stale; worth revisiting once the updated guidance is published.
- **A new scoring criterion or new weights for buying groups.** No source gives a weight, and the weights are already a stated judgement. Folded into the evidence for "Buyer match" instead.
- **A default coverage threshold, such as 3 roles per account.** Forrester reports clients seeing better conversion when sales receives 3 or more buying-group members, but that is client feedback, not a measured benchmark. The skill reports the distribution (1, 2, 3 or more roles) and leaves the target to the agreement with sales.
- **More virtual events.** Forrester's 2026 events survey found a sharp rise in plans to run virtual events. The skill already uses webinars for coverage and nurture and builds each region's mix around the audience, so no change.
- **Events for customer expansion and event platform integration.** Both are in Forrester's findings, and both are already in the skill (expansion is a named job in step 2; same-day lead upload and an end-to-end lead capture test are in the plays and timeline).
- **A script for the in-market check.** The calculation is one division and one multiplication. A script and tests would add upkeep without making the answer more accurate, so the method stays as written guidance with a worked illustration.
- **Fixing the "reussable" typo in the top-level README.** Still present, but outside an agent review; flagged in the pull request.
