# 2026-11-26: paid-media-strategist

**Date:** 26 November 2026
**Agent:** [paid-media-strategist](../paid-media-strategist/)
**Why this agent:** no `improve/` pull requests were open. 3 agents had never been reviewed (paid-media-strategist, pardot-to-hubspot-migration, pipeline-forecast); broken alphabetically, this one comes first.

## What changed

1. **Purchase cycle and the 2 roles of an idea (missing method step).** The market research asked who buys and what triggers a purchase, but never how many buyers are in the market at any one time, and the idea framework had no view on whether an idea builds the brand for future buyers or converts buyers who are ready now. Without that, a report can fill up with search, retargeting and lead form ideas that compete only for the few buyers already looking. The changes:
   - `references/market-research.md`, Part 1: a new step estimates the category's purchase cycle from public evidence (contract lengths, renewal terms, replacement intervals, reviews that mention switching) and turns it into a rough in-market share, showing the arithmetic, labelled Found or Assumed, and described as a rule of thumb rather than a forecast.
   - `references/market-research.md`, Part 2: a new section, "Two roles: brand-building and activation", sets out what each role needs, what the evidence says, and how to use it. Each idea is labelled with its role. A set that is all activation, or all brand-building, needs a reason. Its measure of success has to suit the role. A published ratio is quoted only as context, with its source, and never turned into a budget split, so the skill still produces ideas rather than a plan. The sources are listed at the foot of the file so reports can cite them.
   - `SKILL.md`: steps 2 and 5 and the "Ideas" part of the report structure now ask for the purchase cycle and the role of each idea. In `references/competitor-research.md`, the "funnel emphasis" line now uses the same 2 terms.
2. **A check before delivering (feedback loop).** The workflow ended at "Write the report", with no step to check the draft against the skill's own rules. A new step 7 is a 6-item checklist, covering linked evidence, role and the "different company" test for every idea; platform claims confirmed at source and dated; a screenshot and link for every ad; no invented figures or remembered benchmarks; correct labels; and no plan creeping in. Claude fixes anything that fails, goes through the list again, and delivers only when every item passes or an unfixable gap is stated under "Gaps in the research". Step 6 also gained a one-line body pointing to the report structure and the delivery format.
3. **Platform facts corrected (outdated and contradictory facts).**
   - **Meta Advantage+.** `references/audit-checklist.md` said Advantage+ "is now the default set-up" for new Sales, Leads and App campaigns. `references/channels/meta.md` says Meta's documentation does not say that, so the skill contradicted itself. Meta's Marketing API documentation sets out the actual rule. A campaign shows as Advantage+ only while 3 levers are on: Advantage+ campaign budget, Advantage+ audience in at least one ad set, and Advantage+ placements in every ad set. Placement exclusions set at account level don't switch it off. In the API, placements automation is the default, while budget and audience automation have to be set. The checklist now says this and asks the auditor to check which levers are off and why. The "existing customers" check now notes that the old existing-customer budget cap is not available in new Advantage+ campaigns, and gives Meta's documented replacement: 2 ad sets, one for existing customers with a spending limit and one that excludes them.
   - **LinkedIn Ad Library.** The skill said the library holds ads from "roughly the past year". LinkedIn's help page says it holds ads that ran after 1 June 2023, each kept for 1 year after its last impression. It is searchable by advertiser, payer, keyword, country and date range. Ads targeted to the European Union also show impressions, targeting and the dates they ran, and restricted ads hide the preview and names. The table entry now says this.

`SKILL.md` grows from 116 to 126 lines; the description is unchanged at 869 characters. `references/market-research.md` grows from 63 to 87 lines, still under the 100 lines at which Anthropic suggests a table of contents. The agent has no scripts or tests, so there was nothing to run.

## Why

- John Dawes's 95-5 article (Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, May 2021) shows how to turn an inter-purchase time into an in-market share: a provider changed about every 5 years means about 20% of buyers in the market in a year and about 5% in a quarter, and a 2-year cycle gives 50% and around 13%. He calls the 95% a heuristic. He also argues that buyers rely mostly on memory and, when they do search, prefer brands they know. That is why targeting only buyers who are ready now cannot build a brand. The step gives the agent a way to show this from public evidence rather than assert it.
- Les Binet and Peter Field's "The long and the short of it" (Institute of Practitioners in Advertising presentation) is based on 996 campaigns, 700 brands and 83 categories. It shows brand-building and activation working on different timescales, campaigns that use both kinds of channel producing more brand and business effects than either alone, and campaigns aimed at the whole market recording more very large effects than those aimed only at existing customers. Ideas are what this skill produces, so the role each idea plays belongs in the framework.
- Their report for the B2B Institute (a think tank funded by LinkedIn), written with the same databank, found business-to-business brands grew best with roughly half and half (46% brand-building and 54% activation in LinkedIn's 2019 summary; the Institute's page says 50/50). It recommends 2 targeting approaches in parallel, wide for brand-building and specific for activation, which maps straight onto paid media choices. Thinkbox's page for their later "Effectiveness in Context" says the right balance depends on category, business model and the brand's stage. That is why the skill treats any ratio as context, not a target.
- Anthropic's skill authoring best practices recommend a checklist for complex workflows and a "check, fix, repeat" loop for tasks where quality is critical, including skills without code, where a reference document acts as the checker. This skill already had strong rules (evidence for every idea, platform facts confirmed at source, screenshots, no invented figures, no action plan), but nothing made Claude check the draft against them before delivery.
- Meta's "Advantage+ Campaign Experience for Sales, App, and Leads" documentation and its June 2025 developer blog post give the 3-lever rule, the placements default and the replacement for the existing-customer budget cap. The checklist's "default set-up" sentence matched neither, and contradicted the skill's own Meta primer.
- LinkedIn's Marketing Solutions Help page on the Ad Library gives the coverage dates, the search filters and the extra European Union detail.

## Considered and rejected

- **Putting a budget split in the report.** Binet and Field publish ratios, but the skill deliberately delivers research and ideas, not a plan, and their own later work says the ratio varies by context. The ratio stays as cited context only.
- **Quoting the 60:40 rule for consumer brands.** It appears in the Institute of Practitioners in Advertising presentation as an optimum of about 40% activation. It was left out because the B2B figure, with the caveat that the balance varies, makes the point for this skill's mostly business-to-business uses, and adding a second ratio would invite misuse as a target.
- **Share of voice above share of market as an idea test.** It is the first of the 5 B2B principles, but share of voice means share of advertising spend, which public ad libraries do not show. A research report built from public evidence cannot measure it, so adding it would invite guesses.
- **Category entry points (buying situations) as a message lens.** Well evidenced and relevant, but the marketing-performance-audit agent already covers them, and one method change per review keeps this pull request reviewable. A candidate for a future review.
- **Refreshing the "Recent developments" in each channel primer** (for example, whether Google's ads in AI Mode have reached the United Kingdom). The skill already tells Claude to check official sources at run time and to treat the primers as a starting point. A refresh would date again within weeks, and Anthropic's guidance warns against time-sensitive detail in skills.
- **Stating whether Ads Manager switches Advantage+ on by default for new campaigns.** Meta's Business Help Centre pages could not be read automatically. Third-party blogs disagree, and they are not acceptable sources here. The checklist asks the auditor to look at the levers instead of assuming.
- **Changing the Performance Max negative keyword limit.** Checked against Google's account limits page (10,000 negative keywords per campaign). The skill was already right.
- **Rewriting the description.** At 869 characters it is within the 1,024-character limit, written in the third person, and already covers the triggers, including interview and pitch preparation.
