# Paid media budget reallocation

Across the 5 campaigns with readable history, moving budget without changing the total (£9,187 a week) is predicted to lift conversions from 99.2 to 101.7 a week (**+2%**), taking cost per conversion from £93 to £90.

The last column is the one that matters: budget moves from where the next conversion is most expensive to where it is cheapest, until the two meet or a campaign reaches its limits (30% up or down, and no further than the lowest or highest weekly spend in its history).

**Stopped at the edge of its history.** Beyond the lowest and highest weekly spend a campaign has actually run at, the curve has no weeks to learn from, so a prediction there would be a guess:

- **Meta: Prospecting: lookalike audiences** stopped at £1,877 a week; the lowest weekly spend in its history is £1,860.

To learn whether a bigger move would pay, test a step past that edge for 2 weeks, keeping everything else fixed, then rerun with the new weeks added.

Check before increasing **Google Ads: Search: brand**: brand search is limited by how many people search for you. Look at Search lost impression share (budget): how often the ads were not shown because the budget was too low. If it is close to zero, budget is not what holds this campaign back, so the extra conversions the curve predicts are unlikely to appear, and the curve cannot see that.

## Held at current budget

The history cannot support a reliable recommendation for these campaigns:

- **Google Ads: Performance Max** (£1,200 a week): spend barely varied (0% week to week), so the curve cannot be read.

To make them readable, run a structured spend test: change the budget by about 20% for 2 weeks, then 20% the other way for 2 weeks, keeping everything else fixed.

## Possible waste

- **LinkedIn: Thought leader ads: awareness** (£768 a week): no conversions in the last 4 weeks. Check conversion tracking first; if tracking is sound, pause or rebuild it.

## Before acting

- Move budget in 2 steps and compare actual results with the prediction before taking the second. Judge each step over 2 weeks or 2 conversion cycles, whichever is longer, allowing for conversions that are reported late.
- The curves describe the past. Seasonality, new creative, bidding changes or a competitor's push can all change them.
- Conversions are only comparable if every platform counts them the same way, with similar attribution windows. If one platform counts view-throughs and another does not, fix that first.
