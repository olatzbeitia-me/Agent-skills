# Paid media budget reallocation

Across the 5 campaigns with readable history, moving budget without changing the total (£9,187 a week) is predicted to lift conversions from 99.2 to 101.8 a week (**+3%**), taking cost per conversion from £93 to £90.

## Recommended weekly budgets

| Platform | Campaign | Now | Recommended | Change | Average cost per conversion | Cost of the next conversion: now → after |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Google Ads | Search: generic category terms | £2,792 | £3,617 | +30% | £119 | £147 → £155 |
| Microsoft Ads | Search: generic category terms | £539 | £689 | +28% | £91 | £98 → £99 |
| Google Ads | Search: brand | £1,451 | £1,776 | +22% | £39 | £135 → £156 |
| LinkedIn | Lead gen forms: finance decision makers | £2,203 | £1,553 | -30% | £184 | £240 → £222 |
| Meta | Prospecting: lookalike audiences | £2,202 | £1,552 | -30% | £109 | £185 → £159 |

The last column is the one that matters: budget moves from where the next conversion is most expensive to where it is cheapest, until the two meet or a campaign reaches its change limit (30% up or down).

Check before increasing **Google Ads: Search: brand**: brand search is limited by how many people search for you. If impression share is already high (above about 90%), extra budget mostly raises cost per click rather than conversions, and the curve cannot see that.

## Held at current budget

The history cannot support a reliable recommendation for these campaigns:

- **Google Ads: Performance Max** (£1,200 a week): spend barely varied (0% week to week), so the curve cannot be read.

To make them readable, run a structured spend test: change the budget by about 20% for 2 weeks, then 20% the other way for 2 weeks, keeping everything else fixed.

## Possible waste

- **LinkedIn: Thought leader ads: awareness** (£768 a week): no conversions in the last 4 weeks. Check conversion tracking first; if tracking is sound, pause or rebuild it.

## Before acting

- Move budget in 2 steps, a fortnight apart, and compare actual results with the prediction.
- The curves describe the past. Seasonality, new creative, bidding changes or a competitor's push can all change them.
- Conversions are only comparable if every platform counts them the same way, with similar attribution windows. If one platform counts view-throughs and another does not, fix that first.
