# Regional event norms

Treat these as starting assumptions to confirm with the local sales team and with current research, not as rules. Holiday dates, working weeks and regulations change; check them for the specific year and country.

## 1. Points that apply everywhere

- **Midweek is safest** for in-person business events; Mondays and Fridays usually underperform.
- **Fiscal years differ by country and buyer.** Many companies run January to December, but UK government and many Japanese and Indian organisations run April to March, and Australian organisations commonly run July to June. Buyers with budgets about to reset are easier to move.
- **Hospitality and gifts:** buyers in the public sector and regulated industries (healthcare, financial services) often have strict limits. In the UK, the Bribery Act 2010 means hospitality should be reasonable and proportionate; elsewhere, check local rules and the buyer's own policy.
- **Data protection and follow-up:** in the UK and the European Union, follow-up marketing relies on the attendee's consent or another lawful basis under data protection law (the UK's General Data Protection Regulation and its Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations, and the European Union's General Data Protection Regulation). Canada has its own anti-spam legislation. Build consent into registration and badge scanning.
- **Language:** local-language content and speakers lift attendance and engagement outside international technology audiences. Budget for translation and native-speaking staff.

## 2. United Kingdom and Ireland

- **Concentration:** London dominates for financial services, professional services, media and technology. Manchester, Edinburgh, Birmingham and Leeds matter for some sectors. Dublin hosts the European headquarters of many technology companies.
- **Formats that work:** breakfast briefings (roughly 8:00 to 10:00), roundtables under the Chatham House Rule, dinners in private dining rooms, early-evening receptions. Senior UK audiences respond to peer content and dislike an obvious sales pitch.
- **Quiet periods:** school half-terms, Easter, late July through August, and December from around mid-month.
- **Tone:** understated invitations and content outperform hype.

## 3. Germany, Austria and Switzerland

- **Language:** German-language content and speakers matter, especially outside international technology circles. Switzerland is multilingual (German, French, Italian); Zurich and Geneva are different audiences.
- **Formats:** major trade fairs (Messe) in cities such as Frankfurt, Munich, Düsseldorf, Cologne, Hannover, Hamburg and Berlin carry real weight in industrial and technical sectors. Hosted events favour technical depth and substance over entertainment.
- **Etiquette:** formal invitations sent well ahead, punctual starts, agendas honoured.
- **Data protection:** high sensitivity; be explicit about what happens to contact details.
- **Quiet periods:** German school summer holidays are staggered by state, from late June to mid-September, so check the relevant states; Christmas and Easter; Carnival in the Rhineland (Cologne, Düsseldorf) around February.

## 4. France and the Benelux countries

- **France:** French-language content is strongly expected. Paris concentrates most head offices. Lunches and dinners run longer than in the UK. August is largely closed.
- **Netherlands:** English widely accepted; direct communication style; Amsterdam and Rotterdam.
- **Belgium:** Brussels, with a French and Dutch language split. European institutions bring a public-affairs and policy audience.

## 5. Nordic countries

- **Language:** English widely accepted for business events.
- **Buying style:** flatter hierarchies and consensus-driven decisions, so invite several roles from each account rather than one senior name.
- **Formats:** breakfast events and content-led sessions; low tolerance for hype.
- **Quiet periods:** long summer holidays, particularly July (Sweden especially), plus Christmas.
- **Cities:** Stockholm, Copenhagen, Oslo, Helsinki.

## 6. Southern Europe

- **Spain, Italy, Portugal:** local language strongly preferred; relationship-led buying; later schedules (dinners in Spain start late); August largely closed.
- **Cities:** Madrid and Barcelona; Milan (business) and Rome (public sector); Lisbon.

## 7. Middle East

- **Buyers:** government and government-linked entities are major buyers in the Gulf. Relationships and seniority matter; a local office or partner is often expected for public-sector deals.
- **Formats:** large flagship technology and sector shows in Dubai and Riyadh attract regional buyers; hosted events need a senior host from the company.
- **Calendar:** Ramadan moves roughly eleven days earlier each year; avoid daytime and evening hospitality during it or adapt formats (an iftar gathering can work if hosted with care). Summer heat reduces events from roughly June to August. Working weeks differ across the region (for example, Saudi Arabia works Sunday to Thursday and the United Arab Emirates Monday to Friday); confirm per country.
- **Hospitality:** some venues and audiences expect alcohol-free events.

## 8. North America

- **Scale:** the largest shows and user conferences, often in Las Vegas, San Francisco, New York, Chicago, Orlando and Toronto.
- **Exhibiting costs:** many venues require their official contractors for stand services; some have union labour rules; material handling (often called drayage) is charged by weight and can be a large surprise. Read the exhibitor services manual early.
- **Quiet periods:** Thanksgiving week (late November), the Fourth of July week, and late December.
- **Canada:** anti-spam legislation governs follow-up emails; Quebec expects French.
- **Public sector:** strict gift and hospitality limits for government attendees.
- **Webinars:** pick time slots that work across time zones, or run two sessions.

## 9. Asia-Pacific

- **Singapore:** regional hub for South East Asia; English-language events work well.
- **Australia:** Sydney and Melbourne; summer holidays from mid-December to late January; the July to June financial year affects budget timing.
- **Japan:** Japanese-language content and staff are essential; partner-led selling through resellers and systems integrators is common; relationships take time. Avoid Golden Week (late April to early May) and Obon (mid-August). Fiscal year commonly April to March.
- **India:** Bengaluru, Mumbai, Delhi region, Hyderabad; check Diwali timing each year.
- **China:** treat as a separate strategy with its own regulatory, data and partner considerations.
- **Across the region:** Lunar New Year (January or February) is a quiet period in many markets.

## 10. Latin America

- **Brazil:** Portuguese; São Paulo is the business centre. **Mexico:** Mexico City; Spanish. **Colombia, Chile, Argentina:** Bogotá, Santiago, Buenos Aires.
- **Buying style:** relationship-led; partner-led selling is common.
- **Quiet periods:** Carnival (February or March, especially in Brazil), Easter week, late December to January.
