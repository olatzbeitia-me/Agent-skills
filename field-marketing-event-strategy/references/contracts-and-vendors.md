# Contracts and vendors: terms to negotiate and risks to watch

Practitioner guidance, not legal advice. Larger commitments should go through the company's legal and procurement teams. Get every promise in writing; a sales email from the organiser is not a contract term.

## 1. Sponsorship and exhibitor agreements

**Negotiate:**
- **Deliverables, itemised.** Speaking slot (format, length, time in the programme, who approves the topic), logo placements, passes (how many, which type), meeting rooms, hosted sessions, digital promotion, and exactly what attendee data you receive.
- **Cancellation schedule.** Fees often become non-refundable after a set date. Push for a stepped schedule and for credit against a future edition rather than forfeiture.
- **Organiser cancellation, postponement or move to virtual.** Full refund or the option of one, not just a credit; the right to withdraw if dates, venue or format change materially.
- **Force majeure.** Make sure it covers government restrictions, transport strikes and venue unavailability, and states what happens to fees.
- **Stand location.** Position, size and distance from competitors; many large shows allocate space by points earned from previous participation, so ask how allocation works and whether rebooking on site gives priority.
- **Category exclusivity** for top-tier packages, if competitors would otherwise share the same tier.
- **Payment terms.** Deposit and staged payments rather than full payment on signature.
- **Early-booking deadlines.** Space, speaking submissions and exhibitor services all have them, and missing them costs money.

**Risks to watch:**
- **Hidden exhibiting costs:** power, internet, furniture, rigging, cleaning, material handling, mandatory official contractors, lead capture licences. Ask for the exhibitor services manual before signing.
- **"Attendee list" promises.** Under UK and EU data protection law, organisers can only pass on attendee details with a lawful basis, so the list may be job titles and companies only, or only people who opted in. Know what you are paying for.
- **Restrictions on side events** during official hours, which can block a hosted dinner or breakfast plan.
- **Content approval rights** that let the organiser change or move a speaking slot late.

## 2. Venues for hosted events

**Negotiate:**
- **Minimum spend on food and drink,** room hire and how they interact; whether the minimum is waived if spend reaches a level.
- **Final numbers deadline** (often a few days before) and the tolerance either way.
- **Cancellation schedule** with stepped charges, and a postponement option.
- **Service charge and Value Added Tax** (or local sales tax) stated explicitly, not discovered on the invoice.
- **Audio-visual:** whether you must use the in-house supplier; if so, get an itemised quote.
- **Access times** for setup and breakdown, and any curfew or noise limits.
- **Accessibility** for guests and speakers.
- **Private space guarantee:** a private dining room should be genuinely private for a confidential discussion.

**Risks to watch:** minimum-spend shortfalls charged when attendance drops, exclusive supplier clauses, damage deposits, and insurance requirements the venue will ask for late.

## 3. Hotel room blocks

- **Attrition clauses** charge you for rooms in the block that are not used. Keep the block small and negotiate the attrition percentage.
- **Release dates:** when unsold rooms return to the hotel without penalty.
- Often cheaper and lower-risk to let staff book individually unless rooms will sell out.

## 4. Vendors

Applies to audio-visual suppliers, caterers, event agencies, stand builders, lead capture and registration platforms, promotional items and photographers.

**Negotiate:**
- **Scope in writing,** with deliverables and acceptance criteria (what "done" looks like).
- **Cancellation and change fees,** and the cut-off for changes.
- **Liability and indemnity,** proportionate to the job.
- **Ownership** of stand designs, photography and recordings, and the rights to reuse them.
- **Delivery deadlines** with a buffer before the event, especially for printed and shipped items.

**Risks to watch:** single points of failure (one supplier for the stand, shipping and installation), customs delays for international shipping, and promotional items that breach buyers' gift policies.

## 5. Data protection

- Any vendor that handles personal data on your behalf (registration platform, lead capture app, event agency managing invitations) is a processor and needs a data processing agreement.
- Registration and badge scanning should capture consent for follow-up in a form that will stand up later.
- Know who the data controller is for co-hosted and partner events, and agree in writing how data is shared.

## 6. Insurance

- **Public liability** cover is commonly required by venues.
- **Event cancellation insurance** is worth pricing for large hosted events and big sponsorships with non-refundable fees.
- Check whether the company's existing policies already cover events and international travel.

## 7. Back-scheduled timeline for a major external event

Adjust for the event's own deadlines.

| When | What |
|---|---|
| 6 to 9 months before | Decide tier; negotiate and sign; submit speaking proposals; book space |
| 4 to 5 months | Stand design or side-event venue booked; exhibitor services ordered at early rates; hotels |
| 12 weeks | Target account list agreed with sales; owners named; measurement defined; campaign set up in the customer relationship management system |
| 8 weeks | Pre-event outreach starts; side-event invitations out |
| 4 weeks | Meeting-booking checkpoint with sales; adjust outreach |
| 2 weeks | Staff briefing: messaging, qualification questions, meeting schedule, roles |
| 1 week | Final numbers to venue; lead capture tested end to end; shipping confirmed |
| Event | Daily briefings and debriefs; leads entered same day |
| 1 day after | Hot leads with account owners |
| 2 days after | All leads routed |
| 30, 60, 90 days | Pipeline reviews |
| 6 and 12 months | Pipeline reviews for events whose job is new accounts |
