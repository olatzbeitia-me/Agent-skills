# Sources: what counts as evidence

The skill's knowledge must be grounded in how channels actually work and in what platforms, regulators and official statistics say, not in the blog articles that rank well in search. Many "complete guide" posts are written for search ranking or by AI, repeat each other, and go out of date quietly. In one check (October 2026), several blogs gave September 2026 as the date for Google's Dynamic Search Ads migration; Google's own developer blog had moved it to February 2027 months earlier.

## Order of trust

1. **Official platform sources**: announcements, help centres, developer documentation, product pages.
2. **Official statistics and regulators**: government statistics, regulators' guidance, statutory bodies.
3. **Primary observation**: ad libraries, the company's own website and filings, live search results, all dated.
4. **Reputable trade press**: useful for context and for spotting changes early; label as Reported until confirmed at source.
5. **Industry bodies and research with a stated method**: useful for market context; name the method (sample size, date).

**Do not use as evidence**: SEO content farms, AI-written "guides", undated pages, vendor blogs selling a tool (unless they publish primary data with a method), listicles of "best software". If one of these surfaces something interesting, find the primary source or leave it out.

When an official source and trade press conflict, the official source wins. When an official page can't be read, say so, label the fact Reported, and suggest the person checks it.

## Official statistics and regulators (UK)

| Body | Use for |
|---|---|
| Office for National Statistics | Market size, households, business counts, spending |
| GOV.UK statistics and department publications | Sector statistics and policy |
| Ofcom (Media Nations, Online Nation) | How people use media and platforms, by age |
| Information Commissioner's Office | Cookies, consent and data rules for advertising |
| Advertising Standards Authority / CAP Code | What advertising may claim, sector rules |
| Sector regulators | Financial Conduct Authority, Care Quality Commission, Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency, Gambling Commission and others, as relevant |
| Companies House | Company filings, size, ownership |

## Reputable trade press (label as Reported)

Search Engine Land and Search Engine Roundtable (search advertising changes, often first); Marketing Week; Campaign; The Drum; Digiday; Adweek; The Media Leader; Press Gazette (publishing); PPC Land (fast, but verify). Plus the trade press of the business's own sector (for example Care Home Professional and Homecare Insight for social care).

## Showing evidence in the report

Link every fact to the page it came from, inline, and show every ad as a screenshot with its direct link. Use words only where there is nothing to link: "reported" for trade-press facts not confirmed at source, "(inference)" for your own reasoning, "assumed" for gaps. Date anything time-sensitive. The Sources section groups links as official and primary, ad libraries, and reported.
