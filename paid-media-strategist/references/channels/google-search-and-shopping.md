# Google Search and Shopping (including AI Max, AI Overviews and AI Mode)

## How it works
- Every search triggers an auction among advertisers whose keywords (or automated matching) fit the query. Position is decided by Ad Rank, which combines the bid with quality: expected click-through rate, ad relevance and landing page experience, plus the expected effect of assets and the search context. A better ad can beat a higher bid.
- Advertisers usually pay per click, and the price is generally the minimum needed to hold that position rather than the full maximum bid.
- Search captures demand that already exists. It cannot make people search for something they don't know they need; that is the job of video, social and other demand-creating channels.

## Campaign types and formats
- **Search campaigns**: text ads (responsive search ads with up to 15 headlines and 4 descriptions, combined automatically), plus assets: sitelinks, callouts, structured snippets, images, call, lead form, location, price and promotion.
- **AI Max for Search**: an optional layer on a Search campaign. It expands matching beyond the keyword list (search term matching), rewrites ad text to fit the query (text customisation) and chooses the landing page (final URL expansion). Controls include brand inclusions and exclusions, URL inclusions and exclusions, and location targeting at ad group level. Reports flag which queries came through AI Max. It is replacing Dynamic Search Ads.
- **Shopping**: product ads built from a Merchant Center feed (title, image, price, retailer). Feed quality is the targeting: titles and attributes decide which searches a product can show for. Runs as Standard Shopping or inside Performance Max.
- **Ads in AI Overviews and AI Mode**: Search and Shopping ads can appear above, below or within AI-generated answers. Eligibility comes through AI-powered matching (broad match, AI Max, Performance Max). New formats designed for AI Mode are in testing (see Recent developments).

## Targeting
Keywords with match types (exact, phrase, broad), negative keywords, locations (people *in* a place versus people *interested in* it), language, schedule, devices, and audiences (remarketing lists, Customer Match lists uploaded from the advertiser's own data, in-market and affinity segments) used either to narrow or just to adjust bids.

## Bidding and optimisation
- Automated bidding (Smart Bidding): maximise conversions, maximise conversion value, target cost per acquisition, target return on ad spend. These learn from conversion data, so the conversion definition and volume decide how well they work.
- Manual and simple strategies: manual cost per click, maximise clicks, target impression share (useful for brand defence).
- Broad match and AI Max depend on sound conversion signals; with a poor signal they expand into irrelevant queries.

## Measurement
Google tag or Tag Manager; enhanced conversions (hashed first-party data); offline conversion import from the customer relationship management system; consent mode (needed for UK and European Economic Area users); Google Analytics 4 for cross-channel view; conversion lift and geographic experiments for incrementality.

## Best for and weak for
- **Best for**: businesses whose buyers search with clear intent (services, products people compare, problems people describe); local services; ecommerce with a good feed.
- **Weak for**: new categories with little search volume; brands that need awareness first; very low-margin products in auctions dominated by large retailers.
- **Watch for**: paying for your own brand searches that would arrive organically; competitors bidding on your brand; queries that look relevant but aren't (jobs, free, DIY, login).

## Creative needs
Low production cost (text, images, feed). The craft is in message match: the ad repeating what the person searched for, and the landing page delivering it.

## UK notes
- Financial services advertisers face verification requirements in the UK; check the current Google policy for the products advertised.
- Healthcare, medicines, gambling and alcohol have category policies on top of the ASA's CAP Code.
- Bidding on competitor trademarks as keywords is generally allowed; using them in ad text can be restricted after a complaint.

## Recent developments (checked October 2026)
- **Ads in AI Mode** are scaling in ten countries; the **UK is planned for Q4 2026**, not yet live. Multi-turn ads (in follow-up questions) launching in the US. *Source: Google Ads & Commerce Blog, 28 October 2026.*
- **New AI-era formats** announced at Google Marketing Live (20 May 2026): Conversational Discovery ads and Highlighted Answers being tested in AI Mode; AI-powered Shopping ads (Gemini explains why a product fits) and Business Agent for Leads (a chat agent built from the advertiser's site, replacing a static lead form) "in the coming months"; Direct Offers expanding to promotion bundling and travel deals. No markets given. *Source: Google Ads & Commerce Blog.*
- **Dynamic Search Ads to AI Max**: Google announced the upgrade in 2026; automatic migration was then **delayed to February 2027**, and creating Dynamic Search Ads campaigns was restored. *Source: Google Ads Developer Blog, June 2026.* (Several blogs still quote September 2026; that date is out of date.)
- **Bidding and budgets** (7 May 2026): journey-aware bidding in beta (target cost per acquisition learning from non-biddable conversions such as calls and sign-ups, to follow the lead-to-sale path); Smart Bidding Exploration extending to Shopping and Performance Max; demand-led pacing coming to Search and Shopping; campaign total budgets (a fixed budget over days or weeks) launched earlier in 2026. *Source: Google Ads & Commerce Blog.*
