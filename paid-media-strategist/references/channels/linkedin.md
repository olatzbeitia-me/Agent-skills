# LinkedIn

## How it works
- Auction-based, with a relevance score affecting cost and delivery. LinkedIn's distinctive asset is professional data people maintain about themselves: employer, job title, function, seniority, skills, industry and company size.
- Costs per click and per thousand impressions are typically the highest of the major platforms. That only pays off where the value of reaching a specific professional is high: business-to-business sales, recruitment, professional education, high-value services.

## Campaign types and formats
- Objectives: brand awareness, engagement, video views, website visits, lead generation, website conversions, job applicants.
- Formats: single image, video, carousel and **document ads** in the feed; **Thought Leader ads** (sponsored posts from employees or other members, which tend to earn more trust than company-page ads); event ads; **conversation and message ads** (sponsored messages from a named sender); dynamic ads (follower and spotlight); text ads; **connected TV ads**; **BrandLink** (pre-roll alongside publisher and creator video).
- **Lead gen forms**: pre-filled with profile data. High completion rates, variable intent; qualifying questions help.

## Targeting
Company (names, lists, size, industry, growth), job (title, function, seniority, skills, years of experience), education, member interests and groups, matched audiences (company and contact lists, website and engagement retargeting). Audience expansion and the LinkedIn Audience Network (off-platform inventory) extend reach but dilute precision; test them rather than leaving them on by default.

## Bidding and optimisation
Maximum delivery (automated), cost cap, manual bidding. Small audiences saturate quickly, so frequency and creative rotation matter.

## Measurement
Insight Tag plus Conversions API; customer relationship management system connections (lead sync, revenue attribution reporting); brand lift tests; view-through conversions (report separately).

## Best for and weak for
- **Best for**: business-to-business with a defined buyer (job function, seniority, company size); account-based marketing against target account lists; thought leadership; event promotion; recruitment.
- **Weak for**: consumer products; low-value business-to-business sales where the cost per lead can't be recovered; very small audiences with tiny budgets.

## Creative needs
Medium. What performs is substance: data, opinion, people. Executive and specialist voices (Thought Leader ads), useful documents, short native video.

## UK notes
UK is one of LinkedIn's largest markets. Financial promotions rules apply to regulated products.

## Recent developments (checked October 2026)
- **Connected TV**: three buying routes: in Campaign Manager (brand awareness objective, 15 or 30-second video); **CTV Select** (single premium publishers such as NBCUniversal and Paramount, via a LinkedIn representative); and through demand-side platforms using private marketplace deals. Measurement partners include iSpot, Kantar and Nielsen. *Source: LinkedIn Business, Connected TV Ads page.* The page lists no markets; at launch (April 2024) targeting was US and Canada. **UK availability unconfirmed**; check with LinkedIn before suggesting it.
- **Reported, not verified at source**: BrandLink expansion (publisher pre-roll, bundles with event ads), Top Voices creator sponsorships, programmatic connected TV via The Trade Desk (March 2026), and a managed creative service, BrandWorks (June 2026). *Trade press only.*
